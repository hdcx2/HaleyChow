Treatment Options & Therapies

A plain-language guide to common care approaches

Care depends on the anomaly’s type, flow, location, symptoms, and effect on function. A vascular anomalies team may include interventional radiology, surgery, dermatology, genetics, hematology, or other specialists. Some people are monitored without a procedure; more than one approach may be considered.

Monitoring and symptom care

Follow-up can track changes, symptoms, function, and imaging. Compression or other symptom measures are considered only when appropriate for the person and lesion.

Sclerotherapy

A specialist injects a medicine into selected abnormal channels to reduce or close them. The approach and number of sessions vary by anatomy and lesion type.

Embolization

An image-guided procedure blocks selected abnormal vessels. High-flow lesions require planning by a team experienced with that specific anatomy.

Surgery and laser

Surgery may be considered for selected lesions or complications. Laser treatment can target some superficial color changes. These options are not suitable for every lesion.

Medication and targeted therapy

Some complex conditions may have specialist-directed medical options. Benefits, risks, monitoring, and eligibility depend on the diagnosis and individual circumstances.

Important: This is a general overview, not treatment advice. Do not start, stop, or change treatment based on this page. Ask the treating team which options apply to the exact diagnosis.
