Glossary of Terms

Common words used in genetics and vascular anomaly care

Arteriovenous malformation (AVM)
An abnormal direct connection between arteries and veins. AVMs can occur in different body areas and are not all the same condition.
Capillary malformation
A malformation involving small blood vessels called capillaries, often visible as a skin birthmark.
Embolization
An image-guided procedure that blocks selected blood vessels or abnormal connections.
Germline variant
A genetic change present in reproductive cells and often throughout the body; it may be passed to children, depending on the inheritance pattern.
Lymphatic malformation
A developmental malformation involving lymphatic channels, which carry lymph fluid.
Macrocystic / microcystic
Terms describing larger or smaller fluid-filled spaces within a lymphatic malformation. A lesion can include more than one pattern.
Somatic mosaic variant
A genetic change that occurs after fertilization and is present in some cells or tissues, but not necessarily throughout the body.
Sclerotherapy
A procedure in which a specialist injects an agent into selected abnormal channels to reduce or close them.
Slow-flow / fast-flow
Broad descriptions of blood movement through a vascular malformation. Imaging and specialist assessment help characterize flow.
Venous malformation
A developmental malformation of veins. These are generally slow-flow lesions; their depth, extent, and symptoms vary.
Vascular anomaly
An umbrella term that includes vascular tumors and vascular malformations. Classification matters because they are different groups.
Vascular tumor
A growth involving blood vessels. ISSVA classifies vascular tumors separately from vascular malformations.

Definitions are simplified for learning and do not replace a diagnosis. See the ISSVA Classification & Glossary for the current specialist terminology.
